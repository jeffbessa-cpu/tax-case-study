Case study
01 — Overview

Taxfix case study

The challenge

The UK team's focus this half is growth — getting more of the right people into the product and converting them at the moments that matter. First-time filers are the hardest to convert: anxious, unsure they even need to file, skeptical of a service they've never used, and often arriving close to the 31 January deadline.

The brief: pick one real drop-off in that first-time journey and design to improve it.

  • "Do I even need to file?" — help someone unsure of their liability find out, and see why an assisted service is the safe way to handle it.
  • The commitment moment — make it clear who handles the return, how it works, and what it costs, so they'll actually start.
  • The hand-off moment — once signed up, get them to share the information the advisor needs so the return can move (a common activation drop-off).

Focus beats breadth. The goal was to pick the slice I found most interesting and design it well, not cover the full journey — for desktop web, not a native mobile app.

02 — Scope

The slice I chose

Chosen moment: the commitment moment — right after someone confirms they need to file, before they've committed to Taxfix.

Why this moment?

  • Of the three drop-offs, this is the one where trust is made or lost in a single screen.
    • The other two moments lose people to confusion or friction — fixable with clearer content or fewer steps.
  • This moment loses people to fear and uncertainty — not fixable only by clarifying copy, but by removing the risk itself.
  • If this screen fails, the advisor side of the marketplace never gets a customer to serve.
  • This moment ties directly to this half's stated priority from case study: converting the right people at the moments that matter.

What I deliberately did not design

Eligibility & education flow

Assuming the user already knows they're liable — not designing the "do I need to file" moment.

The advisor's own interface

Only designed a small trust element showing how advisors work — not a full matching or profile system.

The full checkout flow

The payment screen is a simplified placeholder — not a fully designed checkout with validation, error states, or real card processing.

03.1 — Solution

Discovery

Hypothesis

  • At this moment, the majority of users aren't confused by price or process — they fear legal/financial risk (misreporting income, missing the deadline, filing an incorrect return, facing a penalty).

This isn't a checkout step, it's a liability handoff.

Filing the return doesn't mean doing the tax work themselves; they'd rather an expert handle it than risk getting it wrong alone, so they choose an assisted service.

Assumption 02

Users' doubts from the empathy step are "trust questions," not UI questions.

03.3 — Solution

Validation

How to validate this before launching?

1. Comparative message test — fear-framing vs. price-framing

Goal Method Success
Directly test the core hypothesis (fear drives drop-off) against the leading alternative (price drives drop-off), rather than assuming it. Two versions of the commitment screen — one leading with responsibility/trust language, one leading with price/value — shown to matched first-time filers; ask which they'd trust more and why.

A majority preference for the trust-framed version, with reasoning that references risk/responsibility rather than cost.

A split or price-driven preference would be a signal to revisit the hypothesis, not just the copy.

2. Validate the problem and solution

Goal Method Success
Confirm that the commitment moment is actually where users hesitate. Use prototype as conversation vehicle.
  • Review funnel analytics to identify the biggest drop-off point.
  • Analyse support tickets or live chat conversations for recurring questions.
  • Interview 5–8 first-time filers who considered using a tax advisor.
  • If trust and uncertainty consistently emerge as major barriers, it validates that this is the right problem to solve.
  • Ask users to evaluate confidence level from 0-10 and use it as a metric
03.4 — Solution

Iterations

Ideas for future

Room For Improvement

Idea 01: Advisor pricing could be offered in tiers: Basic advisor VS Full package or based on business size

This assumption needs to be tested (AB test or individual user testing), if offering 2+ tiers options brings more insecurity to user's decision at this point or fasten conversion.

Idea 02: A pre-advisor assignment could happen in the first step, bringing user closer to their advisor 1 step earlier. Bringing more reliability and human-like feeling to first step.

This assumption needs to be checked against if advisors can be "pre-assigned" based on the teams capacity, without overloading/bringing complexity to admin views.

Idea 03: Make it look like more marketplace style with brand elements — advisor cards, ratings, and Taxfix's visual identity throughout the flow, so it feels like a curated service rather than a generic checkout.

Not the goal for this case study.

Idea 04: Add a support chat bubble in the How it works sub section to answer questions from users during the commitment flow, as a way to reduce risk feel from users.

This assumption needs to be tested against whether live/AI chat support at this step actually reduces hesitation, or just adds another distraction before commitment.

05 — Outcome

Success criteria

How to validate solution success?

Run an A/B experiment

Goal: Evaluate which flow has a better conversion rate

Method: A/B Experiment: Current commitment experience VS New redesigned experience

Primary metric: Commitment Conversion Rate.

Secondary metric: Completion of onboarding, Time spent on the page, CTA click-through rate, Bounce rate

Success: Statistically meaningful lift in conversion at this step for the new version, with no offsetting drop in later-funnel trust (e.g. refund/complaint rates), confirming the new experience helps rather than just feels better.
